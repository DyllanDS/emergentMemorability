D' BY PRECEDING-CONTEXT SIMILARITY, STANDARDIZED FOR POSITION AND LAG
========================================================================================================
6,223,953 trials, 21,137 participants, 26,107 images.
Context = ctx_top10: the mean of the ten highest SPoSE correlations between the probe
and the items already shown in that participant's stream, excluding prior showings of
the probe itself. The measure, and the predecessor window it runs over, are identical
for old and new trials; only the outcome differs.
Trials require at least 20 preceding items. Bins are within-image rank bins
(20 of them), so each image contributes equally to every bin.
Standardization: 10 serial-position strata for false alarms and 10x10 position-by-lag strata
for hits, each bin re-weighted to the same stratum distribution, so bins are compared
at the same positions and lags.
Confidence intervals are 400 bootstrap resamples of participants.

STEP 4. MODEL-BASED ROUTE: per-participant regressions with position and lag entered
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Each participant contributes one slope on the context-bin index (1-20, centered within
image), with serial position, and lag for hits, entered as continuous covariates.
Inference is across participants. Slopes are in probability per bin step; predicted
rates at bin 1 and bin 20 come from moving 19 bin steps around each measure's overall
rate, and those predicted rates are converted to d'. Run on the adjusted bins, this is
the same contrast as step 2 by a route that assumes linearity instead of relying on
stratum overlap.

STEP 6. ROBUSTNESS: adjusted bins defined on one common within-image distribution
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Above, old and new trials are ranked separately within image, so bin 20 means the most
crowded twentieth of this image's repeats and, separately, of its first showings. Here
both are ranked against the same pooled within-image distribution, so a bin index
refers to the same absolute context level for hits and false alarms. Image composition
is then no longer exactly balanced across bins.
