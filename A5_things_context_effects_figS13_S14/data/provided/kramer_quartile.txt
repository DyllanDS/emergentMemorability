KRAMER ET AL.'S QUARTILE TEST AND THE WITHIN-IMAGE CONTEXT EFFECT, SAME TRIALS
====================================================================================================
6,223,953 trials, 21,137 participants, 26,107 images.

Kramer et al. (2023) split participants into quartiles by how many exemplars of a
category they saw, scored that category's images separately within the top and the
bottom quartile, correlated the two score vectors, and compared the score
distributions. That test is reproduced here on the same trial-level data.
Alongside it, in the same participants and the same images, is our test: bin each
image's trials by how crowded the preceding stream was in feature space (mean
similarity to the top10 nearest preceding items, binned WITHIN image so
image identity is held constant) and read off d' from the least to the most crowded bin.

Corrected recognition (hit rate minus false alarm rate) is kept as Kramer et al.
defined it for their test.

WHOLE DATASET, ALL CATEGORIES: where the within-image effect lives
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  d' least-crowded bin 3.214 -> most-crowded bin 2.705   (drop 0.508)
  hit rate 0.8426 -> 0.7905     false alarm rate 0.0136 -> 0.0289
  of the 0.508 drop in d', 0.197 (39%) comes from hits and 0.311 (61%) from false alarms

  across the 26,107 images with at least 20 trials of each kind, corrected
  recognition correlates r = +0.987 with hit rate and r = -0.077 with false alarm rate;
  the mean false alarm rate is 0.0200.
  Across images, corrected recognition therefore follows the hit rate far more closely
  than the false alarm rate.

NOTES
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Hit and false alarm trials are different trials, binned separately within image and
combined per bin, so d' pairs rates from matched but not identical trials. Similarity
is at the concept level, since SPoSE embeddings are per concept.
