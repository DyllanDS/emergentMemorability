PAIRED WITHIN-IMAGE CROWDING CONTRAST
============================================================================================

WHAT THIS SHOWS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Each of the 1,023 images measured in two or more sets is compared with itself:
  the set where it sat among the least similar company versus the set where it sat
  among the most similar company. Every image contributes exactly one difference and
  every image counts the same amount.

  d-prime is higher by 0.280 [0.239, 0.320] when the same photograph sits
  in its less crowded set. Hits rise by 0.027 [0.019, 0.034] and false alarms fall by
  0.041 [0.035, 0.047]; in d-prime units, 0.079 of the change comes from the hits and
  0.201 from the false alarms. Section 3 below gives the same contrast for the model's
  predicted rates.

  On the same pairs the model predicts +0.097 in d-prime, against +0.280 observed (35% of
  the observed change). In the model, false alarms carry 0.169 in d-prime (its total change
  is 0.097), and it predicts hits falling in the less crowded set (-0.014), while the
  observed hit rate rose.

============================================================================================
1. THE PAIRS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  4,690 image-by-set cells, 3,544 distinct images.
  1,023 images appear in two or more sets: 900 in two sets, 123 in three.
  Crowding measure: crowd_near (higher = closer neighbors = more crowded).
  Cells rebuilt from the trial files: largest difference in trial count 0, in rate 0.00e+00.
  Participants in datasets_test.csv (450 of them, each in exactly one set).

2. PAIRED CONTRAST: least crowded set minus most crowded set, same image
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Images weighted equally. CIs are 2.5/97.5 percentiles of 10,000 image resamples.

                 measure     mean        95% CI (images)
                hit rate   +0.027       [+0.019, +0.034]
        false alarm rate   -0.041       [-0.047, -0.035]
                 d-prime   +0.280       [+0.239, +0.320]

3. THE MODEL ON THE SAME PAIRS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Model predicted rates for the same cells, contrasted over the same pairs, images
  weighted the same way.

                 measure                   observed                      model
                hit rate    +0.027 [+0.019, +0.034]    -0.014 [-0.016, -0.011]
        false alarm rate    -0.041 [-0.047, -0.035]    -0.032 [-0.036, -0.028]
                 d-prime    +0.280 [+0.239, +0.320]    +0.097 [+0.085, +0.108]

  Base rates over the paired cells: observed hit 0.719 FA 0.125, model hit 0.720 FA 0.111.
  Split of the paired d-prime effect: observed 0.079 from hits and 0.201 from false
  alarms; model -0.040 from hits and 0.169 from false alarms.

  The model's rates carry no item-level intrinsic memorability term, so its paired
  changes come only from the other images in each set. The split of the d-prime
  change holds each rate at its mean over the paired cells (the base rates above)
  and moves it half its mean change either side, so the two parts of the split
  need not add up to the mean of the per-image d-prime changes in the table above
  (the 1/(2N) limits use the mean numbers of old and new trials of those cells).
